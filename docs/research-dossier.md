# Barking Raw Store — Copy Research Dossier

Compiled 2026-07-15. Four parallel research passes, every claim sourced, with honesty
flags marking where common raw/natural-pet marketing overreaches and would be debunked.

**Confidence key:** **Solid** = strong peer-reviewed/regulatory backing, safe to state plainly.
**Reasonable** = real and defensible but needs careful wording. **Contested / Myth-to-avoid** =
commonly repeated but exaggerated, unproven, or wrong. Do not use as stated.

**The golden rule for this brand:** frame around *what we deliberately leave out* and *the
transparency of what's in* (fully defensible, sourced), NOT around *kibble/treats actively
harming/killing dogs* (mostly unproven in dogs, easily attacked). The deception is provable;
the disease-causation is not.

## PART 1 — What's actually in mainstream UK supermarket treats

Underpinning fact: UK/EU law lets manufacturers declare ingredients by **category**
("cereals", "meat and animal derivatives") rather than by named ingredient (a "closed"
declaration). Premium brands use "open" declaration (naming "dried chicken" etc.). That
contrast is our single strongest, fully-defensible line.

### Real label facts (all quoted from manufacturer / major-retailer pages)

- **Bonio Original (Purina):** "Cereals (of which wholegrain 34%), Derivatives of vegetable
  origin, Oils and fats, Meat and animal derivatives, Minerals." Cereals are ingredient #1;
  meat is a distant fourth and unnamed. Source: https://www.purina.co.uk/dog/dog-food/product-bonio-original-biscuits
- **Pedigree Markies:** "Cereals, meat and animal derivatives (including 10% marrowbone),
  various sugars, oils and fats, minerals, derivatives of vegetable origin." **Sugar ("various
  sugars") is the 3rd ingredient.** Source: https://pet-supermarket.co.uk/products/pedigree-markies-adult-dog-treats-marrowbone-biscuits
- **Pedigree Jumbone (Medium, Beef & Poultry):** meat spelled out as **"equivalent to Beef 2%
  and Poultry 2%"**, with **"Various Sugars" as the 3rd ingredient, above the meat.** Protein
  only 7%. Source: https://www.uk.pedigree.com/dog-food/dog-treats/pedigree-jumbone-medium-dog-treats-beef-and-poultry-2-chews
- **Pedigree Dentastix (Medium):** "Cereals*, Derivatives of Vegetable Origin, Minerals
  (including Sodium Tripolyphosphate 2.4%), Meat and Animal Derivatives*, Oils and Fats." The
  "beef" and "chicken" are **sensory additives: Beef flavour 5.8 mg/kg, Chicken flavour 9.7
  mg/kg** — milligrams per kilo of flavouring. Fundamentally a cereal stick. Source:
  https://www.uk.pedigree.com/dog-food/dog-dental-care/pedigree-dentastix-daily-dental-chews-medium-dog-treat-2-7kg-1-44kg-720g-128g-77g
- **Bakers Beef with Vegetables (Purina, current recipe):** "Cereals (wholegrains* 59%) Meat
  and animal derivatives (of which 4% beef) ... Glycerol ... Vegetables** (of which 0.3% dried
  pea and 0.3% dried carrot)..." **59% cereals, 4% beef, 0.3% pea + 0.3% carrot**, plus
  glycerol humectant. Source: https://www.purina.co.uk/dog/dog-food/product-bakers-dry-adult-beef-vegetables
- **Winalot Shapes (Purina):** "Cereals (of which wholegrain 19%), Oils and fats, Derivatives
  of vegetable origin, Meat and animal derivatives, Minerals, Vegetables." Cereals #1, oils #2,
  meat #4. Source: https://www.purina.co.uk/dog/dog-food/product-winalot-shapes-biscuits
- **Wagg Meaty Goodness (budget exemplar):** "Meat and Animal Derivatives (25% of which 0.5%
  Chicken, 0.5% Lamb and 0.5% Beef), Cereals, ... Glycerol, Various Sugars..." 25% derivatives
  but only 1.5% named meat. Source: https://waggfoods.com/products/wagg-adult-dog-food-with-beef-veg

### Label-law glossary (the copy gold), from FEDIAF Code of Good Labelling Practice

- **"Meat and animal derivatives"** — legally: all fleshy parts of slaughtered warm-blooded
  land animals plus products/derivatives of processing the carcase. Need not name species or cut.
- **"Cereals"** — umbrella for wheat/maize/oats/rice/barley, no requirement to say which or how much.
- **"Various sugars"** — group term allowing added sugars without naming the source.
- **Named-ingredient % rules:** "beef **flavour**" = **under 4%** beef; "**with** beef" = at
  least 4%; "**rich in** beef" = at least 14%; "beef **dinner/meal**" = at least 26%.
- Sources: FEDIAF Layman's Guide https://europeanpetfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/layman_s_guide_FEDIAF_Labelling_Code_October_2018.pdf
  ; FEDIAF Code 2019 https://europeanpetfood.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/FEDIAF_labeling_code_2019_onlineOctober2019.pdf
  ; UK Pet Food https://www.ukpetfood.org/new-trade-zone/codes-of-practice/labelling.html

### Honesty flags
- **Bakers artificial colours (tartrazine etc.) = HISTORICAL.** Older recipes had azo dyes;
  Purina reformulated them out (main meals by 2020, snacks by 2023). Say "used to", never
  "does". Source: https://www.purina.eu/our-impact/our-commitments/remove-artificial-colourants
- US "Gravy Bones" (Milk-Bone: caramel colour, BHA/BHT, meat-and-bone-meal) is a *different*
  product from UK Pedigree Biscrok. Use only to illustrate what vague terms can cover.
- No distinct UK supermarket own-brand biscuit list verified (they mostly stock Bonio itself).
- Never invent a sugar percentage — brands don't publish one. You can say sugar is added and
  ranked 3rd (true for Markies and Jumbone).

## PART 2 — Processing chemistry (frame as "what we leave out")

- **Extrusion:** kibble is cooked at roughly **125–150°C** under pressure. Solid. Source:
  Frontiers in Vet Science 2026 https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2026.1690420/full
- **Maillard / AGEs:** high heat forms Advanced Glycation End-products (CML, HMF). van Rooijen
  et al. 2014 (J Agric Food Chem): dogs' HMF intake ~122x higher than adult humans'.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25088431/ . Bridglalsingh et al. 2024: more processing = more
  dietary AGEs, raising plasma AGEs in dogs. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11327896/
  - **CRITICAL FLAG:** in both studies **canned/wet food scored HIGHER for AGEs than kibble.**
    Say "heat-processed food" broadly, not "kibble is uniquely worst". Raw/gently-cooked being
    lowest is the true selling point.
  - **Health-effect flag:** AGE harms shown in humans; long-term dog consequences NOT proven.
    Say "linked to inflammation and ageing in humans; research in dogs is still emerging."
- **Acrylamide:** high-heat cooking of starchy ingredients can form it; detected in kibble at
  low, variable levels. Fair: "can form acrylamide, not present in raw food." Don't imply high
  danger. Sources: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpn.70053
- **Nutrient loss:** heat lowers some amino-acid (lysine) availability; makers fortify to
  compensate — i.e. added synthetic nutrients *because* processing strips them. Don't over-quantify.

### Propylene glycol — the "antifreeze" angle, handle exactly
- PG (E1520) is a humectant in some semi-moist treats. Actual antifreeze is **ethylene glycol**,
  a *different, highly toxic* chemical. PG and EG are both glycols but NOT the same substance.
- **FAIR to say:** "a close chemical relative of the glycol used in antifreeze"; "FDA classes
  it GRAS for dog food but **banned it from cat food** (since 1996) because it causes Heinz-body
  anaemia in cats"; "we never use it."
- **MUST NOT say:** "it is antifreeze" / "same as antifreeze" (that's ethylene glycol — wrong).
- Sources: https://www.dvm360.com/view/propylene-glycol-educate-yourself-and-your-veterinary-clients
  ; https://www.dogfoodadvisor.com/red-flag-ingredients/propylene-glycol-dog-food-aid-or-automotive-anti-freeze/
  ; https://www.revivalanimal.com/learning-center/propylene-glycol-and-cats

### Preservatives
- **Ethoxyquin — strongest clean line:** a synthetic preservative the **EU suspended (2017,
  Reg 2017/962) then withdrew (2022, Reg (EU) 2022/1375)** from animal feed because safety
  couldn't be confirmed. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2022/1375/oj/eng
- **BHA/BHT:** EU-authorised up to 150 mg/kg; BHA is IARC Group 2B (possible carcinogen) from
  high-dose rodent studies. Say "linked to cancer in lab animals at high doses; we preserve
  naturally with vitamin E." Don't say "cause cancer in dogs." Sources:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009650/ ; https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2022.7286
- **Overarching flag:** a 2025 meta-analysis (121 studies) found approved additives at permitted
  levels showed no measurable adverse effects. Defensible position = *avoidance/preference*, not
  *danger*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12784728/

### Ultra-processed vs whole
- Kibble IS ultra-processed by manufacturing criteria (Solid). But "ultra-processed = proven
  harm in dogs" is NOT established; the human UPF definition doesn't map cleanly onto formulated
  pet diets. Fair framing: "a growing body of research is questioning ultra-processed pet food,
  and vets are calling for a proper classification system." Lead on transparency + the things
  genuinely different in raw/whole food (lower AGEs, no synthetic preservatives, no humectants,
  no acrylamide, minimal nutrient degradation). Avoid disease-causation claims.

## PART 3 — The biology (our debunk-proof high ground)

- **Short, simple gut** built for meat, not lengthy plant fermentation. Solid anatomy; skip the
  fake precise multipliers ("2ft vs 100ft").
- **Highly acidic stomach (~pH 1–2)**, evolved as a microbial filter for carrion/raw meat.
  Beasley et al. 2015, PLOS ONE. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0134116
  Say "around pH 1–2", not "battery acid".
- **Shearing teeth** (carnassials) for tearing, not grinding. Solid anatomy; frame as "built to
  tear meat", not "proof of obligate carnivore".
- **No salivary amylase** — not built to start digesting starch in the mouth. Solid. But dogs DO
  make pancreatic amylase, so don't spin this into "can't digest starch".
- **THE CARNIVORE LINE — GET RIGHT:** dogs are **facultative/adaptable carnivores, omnivore-
  leaning**, NOT obligate carnivores (cats are). MSD Vet Manual. Dogs evolved *increased* starch
  digestion vs wolves (AMY2B, ~7x more copies): Axelsson et al. 2013, Nature.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11837 . **Do not say "dogs are wolves / obligate
  carnivores."** Aim fire at ultra-processing and cereal filler, not carbs in principle.
- **Defensible copy line:** "Dogs aren't wolves — they evolved alongside us and can digest some
  starch. But they're still built, from teeth to gut, primarily for meat, and thrive on
  meat-rich, minimally-processed food."
- **Gut/stool:** a meat-rich diet improved stool quality and microbiome diversity (Sandri et al.
  2017, BMC Vet Res — 8 Boxers, small). Say "studies suggest". Firmer-stool finding is the most
  concrete. https://bmcvetres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12917-017-0981-z
- **Raw pathogen caveat:** raw meat carries Salmonella/Listeria risk. If our products are
  cooked/air-dried treats, talk "natural, single-ingredient, meat-first", not raw-feeding health
  claims.
- **Dental:** natural chews reduce tartar (Marx et al. 2016, Aust Vet J) BUT do not prevent gum
  disease and hard bones can fracture teeth; VOHC-approved processed chews do work. Say "chewing
  helps keep teeth cleaner, not a substitute for brushing/vet dental care." https://vohc.org/

## PART 4 — Per-product benefits (feeds product descriptions)

Two cross-cutting truths: (1) glucosamine/chondroitin is real on *composition* but oral
bioavailability is low and outcome trials are mixed — say "a natural source of glucosamine &
chondroitin", never "treats arthritis". (2) **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) is the strongest evidence base**
— real randomised vet trials (Roush et al., JAVMA) — making sprats and salmon our most defensible
functional claims. https://avmajournals.avma.org/view/journals/javma/236/1/javma.236.1.59.xml

1. **Beef trachea rings** — cartilage, high protein, low fat, digestible; natural glucosamine/
   chondroitin. Caveat: gulp risk for big chewers; air-dried single-ingredient only.
2. **Chicken feet** — collagen + natural glucosamine; crunchy, good starter chew. Caveat: only
   raw/dehydrated, never cooked (bones splinter); chicken allergen.
3. **Rabbit ears** — lean, novel-ish protein, fibrous roughage. **DROP "natural dewormer"** — no
   evidence, vets reject it. Never a substitute for a worming programme.
4. **Rabbit feet (fur on)** — lean, low-fat, novel protein for sensitivities; small crunchy
   treat. Same "dewormer" myth to avoid. Supervise (small bones).
5. **Duck wings** — raw meaty bone, softer than chicken bone; calcium/phosphorus; joint cartilage.
   Safety-critical: **never cooked** (splinters), raw/air-dried only, higher fat, for confident
   chewers used to raw meaty bones.
6. **Tripe sticks (dried)** — sell on protein + palatability. **Drying degrades the probiotics/
   enzymes** raw green tripe is praised for — keep that claim for raw/freeze-dried only.
7. **Whole sprats (dried)** — champion: EPA/DHA omega-3 (skin, coat, joints, brain) with genuine
   RCT support; whole-fish vitamins. Caveat: oily/calorie-dense, feed in moderation.
8. **Salmon bites** — omega-3 (skin/coat/joints), lean high-value training treat. **Safety: never
   raw** (salmon poisoning disease — fluke-borne, often fatal); cooked/air-dried/freeze-dried
   only. https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/salmon-poisoning
9. **Pure meat tit-bits** — single-ingredient, low-calorie, high-value training reward; nutrient-
   dense (esp. organ). Caveat: if liver/organ, don't over-feed (vitamin A); keep treats to ~10%
   of daily calories.

### Lead-with-confidence claims
Omega-3 skin/coat/joint benefits of **sprats and salmon**; single-ingredient low-calorie training
value of **pure meat tit-bits**; lean novel-protein positioning of **rabbit**; digestible-protein
and palatability of **tripe**.

### Myths to avoid entirely
Rabbit "natural dewormer"; glucosamine chews "treat arthritis"; probiotics/enzymes on *dried*
tripe; any "raw salmon" framing; "cleans teeth" as a substitute for dental care; "it IS
antifreeze"; "these additives are harming/killing your dog".
